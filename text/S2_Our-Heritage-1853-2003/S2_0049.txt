N writing this history of the San Francisco Ladies’ Protection and Relief Society, I
owe a great debt to two authors who have told the earlier parts of this story in the
past, Rowena Beans and Carol Green Wilson. These and a few other useful sources

are listed below.

Beans, Rowena. “Inasmuch...” The One Hundred-Year History of the San Francisco
Ladies’ Protection and Relief Society, 1853-1953. San Francisco: Ladies’ Protection and
Relief Society, 1953.

Ethington, Philip J. The Public City: The Political Construction of Urban Life in San
Francisco, 1850-1900. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1994.

Ginzberg, Lori D. Women and the Work of Benevolence: Morality, Politics, and Class in the
Nineteenth-Century United States. New Haven: Yale University Press, 1990.

Lockwood, Charles. Suddenly San Francisco: The Early Years of an Instant City. San
Francisco: The San Francisco Examiner Division of the Hearst Corporation, 1978.

Lotchin, Roger W. San Francisco 1846-1856: From Hamlet to City. New York: Oxford
University Press, 1974.

Olasky, Marvin. “The Rise and Fall of American Orphanages,” in Richard B.
McKenzie, ed., Rethinking Orphanages for the 21st Century. Thousand Oaks, CA:
Sage Publications, 1998.

Soulé, Frank, et al. The Annals of San Francisco. Berkeley: Berkeley Hills Books, 1999.
(Originally published in San Francisco, 1855.)

Wilson, Carol Green. A History of The Heritage, 1853-1970. San Francisco: Ladies’
Protection and Relief Society, 1970.
