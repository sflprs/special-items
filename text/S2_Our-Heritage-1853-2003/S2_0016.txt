As the Society’s home
was taking shape, a new
focus was taking shape as
well. The Society had begun
in response to women in
peril, and it still assisted
many women. Increasingly,
however, children were
becoming the focus of the

Society’s work. In 1860, roughly equal numbers of women and children resided
in the Society’s home. By 1862, the number of children living in the home had
risen to more than twice the number of women residents. The Ladies’ Home
was soon almost exclusively a home for children, with only a few elderly women

living there.
