There was no shortage of children to
care for. The home’s population exceeded
one hundred by the end of the 1860s,
and its capacity grew to approach three
hundred children, ranging in age from
infancy to fifteen, at any one time. The
Ladies’ Home family was impressively
diverse for its time, as the Society made
a point of accepting children with-
out regard to race or creed. (See the
